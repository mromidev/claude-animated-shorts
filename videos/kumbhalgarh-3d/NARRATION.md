# Kumbhalgarh: The Great Wall of India (narration, English)

Facts (Wikipedia "Kumbhalgarh" and the UNESCO "Hill Forts of Rajasthan" listing):
- Built in the 15th century by Rana Kumbha of Mewar; of the 84 forts in his dominion he is said to have built 32.
- On a hilltop about 1,100 m above sea level in the Aravalli range; perimeter walls extend 36 km; frontal walls 15 feet thick.
- Seven fortified gateways; over 360 temples inside (about 300 Jain, the rest Hindu).
- Badal Mahal ("Palace of Clouds") stands at the top. Maharana Pratap was born here in 1540.
- Legend: the wall would not stand until a holy man gave his life; a shrine marks the main gate (Hanuman Pol).
- Tradition: Kumbha burned massive lamps (50 kg ghee, 100 kg cotton) to light the valley for farmers at night.
- The fort is said to have fallen to direct assault only once, to Akbar's forces, after its drinking water ran short.
- UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2013. "Eight horses abreast" and "Great Wall of India" are popular descriptions.

```
1. High in the Aravalli hills of Rajasthan stands a wall so long, it is often called the Great Wall of India.
2. This is Kumbhalgarh. Its ramparts run for thirty-six kilometres, over ridge after ridge.
3. It was built in the fifteenth century by Rana Kumbha, king of Mewar. He is said to have raised thirty-two forts. This was his masterpiece.
4. Legend says the wall kept falling, until a holy man offered his own life. Where he fell, the great gate was raised.
5. The walls are up to fifteen feet thick. Wide enough, they say, for eight horses to ride side by side.
6. Seven gates guard the climb. And within the walls stand more than three hundred and sixty temples.
7. At the very top sits Badal Mahal, the Palace of Clouds. In the monsoon, the clouds drift right past its windows.
8. Here, in fifteen forty, Maharana Pratap was born. The warrior king who would never bow to an empire.
9. Every night, it is said, Kumbha burned giant lamps, to light the valley for farmers working after dark.
10. The fort is said to have fallen only once, to the armies of the Mughal emperor Akbar. And only after its water ran short.
11. Five centuries on, it is a World Heritage Site. And the great wall of Kumbhalgarh still rides the hills.
```
